Everything has a coach now: your calorie counter, your watch, your workout log. In about a year, "AI coach" went from a rare feature to a headline on the App Store pages of some of the biggest health apps. What that headline doesn't tell you is what the coach can actually see, and that decides whether it's talking about you or about people in general.

A note on bias. We make Fimbl, and its coach is an eating and habits coach, not a personal trainer. Every claim about another app was checked against that company's own website, help centre, App Store listing or a named news report in November 2026. "Not documented" means we couldn't find the feature there, not that it definitely doesn't exist.

"AI fitness coach" can mean three different things

Search for an AI fitness coach and you'll get three kinds of app under one label. Knowing which one you need saves you from paying for the wrong coach.

  • Workout coachesBuild and adjust your training plan. Fitbod is the classic example. They rarely know what you ate.
  • Body-data coachesRead a wearable's sleep, strain and recovery data. Think WHOOP Coach and Google Health Coach.
  • Eating coachesRead your food log and goals. Fimbl, MyFitnessPal, Cronometer and Noom sit here, each in its own way.

If your goal is building muscle or running faster, start with the first two. If your goal is losing weight, you want a coach that can see your food, because a coach that can't see your plate can only talk in generalities.

The coaches, and who each one suits

  • Best all-round health coach

    Google Health Coach

    Built with Gemini, it aims to be a fitness trainer, sleep coach and wellbeing adviser in one. It builds a weekly workout plan, reads your sleep, and analyses photos of your meals. The Fitbit app is now Google Health, on iPhone and Android.5, 6

    Keep in mind: it needs Google Health Premium, is available only in certain countries, and launched first for Fitbit and Pixel Watch owners.

  • Best for strength workouts

    Fitbod

    AI-generated gym workouts that balance your exercises, track muscle recovery and learn from your edits. If what you want is someone to tell you what to lift today, this is the one to try.7

    Keep in mind: a subscription after a free trial, and its listing doesn't document food logging or a chat coach.

  • Best for recovery data

    WHOOP Coach

    Ask questions and get on-demand answers based on your own biometric data: sleep, strain, recovery and stress. Hard to match if you're training for something specific and already wear the strap.8

    Keep in mind: the app requires a WHOOP wearable and membership, and it isn't built around what you eat.

  • Best on top of a huge database

    MyFitnessPal Coach

    Launched in June 2026, Coach turns your logged meals, goals and habits into nutrition guidance: food swaps, recipes, portion changes and restaurant picks. It can also answer questions about workouts.3, 4

    Keep in mind: Premium and Premium+ only, in the US, UK, Canada, Australia and New Zealand.

  • Best for a real person too

    Noom

    Welli, Noom's AI assistant, gives fast, general answers on food, fitness and the programme. New US members can add a Premium tier with a dedicated human coach who checks in weekly, plus meal and exercise plans.9

    Keep in mind: Premium is billed separately from the Noom Weight subscription and is US-only. Noom describes Welli's answers as general.

  • Best for nutrient-minded eaters

    Cronometer Crono Coach

    An AI assistant in Cronometer Gold that reviews your diary, nutrient averages and targets to spot patterns, and you can tell it what to watch, like a fibre target. Cronometer says it won't change your diary or targets by itself.10

    Keep in mind: Gold only, and it's built around nutrition data rather than hunger or mood.

  • Best hands-off numbers

    MacroFactor

    Less a chat than an algorithm: it estimates your energy use from your weight trend and logged food, and adjusts your calorie and macro targets every week, however closely you followed them.11

    Keep in mind: no free tier, and no chat coach documented.

  • Best AI coach for losing weight through eating, on iPhone

    Keep in mind: iPhone and iPad only, the coach is part of Premium, and it doesn't write workout programmes.

Four questions to ask any AI coach

A confident, friendly answer is the easy part. Any modern AI can produce one. These questions show whether there's substance behind it.

  1. What can it actually read? Your food, your workouts, your sleep, how you felt? A coach can only be as personal as what it sees, so look for a clear list.
  2. Does it end in something to do? Good coaching narrows things down to one small change you can try this week, not a wall of general advice.
  3. Does it know its limits? An AI coach isn't a doctor, dietitian or therapist. It should say so, and point you to a person when it matters.
  4. Where does your data go? Food, mood and body data are personal. Ask whether AI is optional, what gets sent, and whether it's used to train models.

Does AI coaching actually work?

Honestly, it's too early to say much. A 2021 systematic review of AI chatbots for activity, diet and weight loss couldn't reach definitive conclusions, and found only one study that measured weight objectively.1 A 2023 meta-analysis was more encouraging about chatbots for physical activity, fruit and vegetable intake and sleep, but most of its studies were short and of weak quality, and it didn't look at body weight.2 We didn't find a large peer-reviewed trial showing that today's generative AI coaches lead to more weight loss.

The better-tested idea is simpler. Self-monitoring is one of the most consistent habits linked to weight loss, so a good way to think about a coach is as a reason to keep logging, and as a way to get something useful back when you do. The best coach isn't the one that sounds smartest. It's the one that gives you a reason to come back tomorrow.

Can ChatGPT be your fitness coach?

For knowledge, it's very good. A general AI assistant can explain progressive overload, sketch a beginner's gym plan or suggest high-protein lunches, and it's a great place to start. Its limit is that it mostly knows what you tell it. It doesn't log your meals, it doesn't remember to ask on Thursday how Tuesday went, and copying your diary into a chat gets old quickly. Some assistants can now connect to health apps if you allow it, so check what that covers and what happens to your data.

A dedicated coach trades range for context: it can do less, but it already has your week in front of it. Plenty of people will use both, and that's a perfectly good answer.
