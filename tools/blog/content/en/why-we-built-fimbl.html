Anyone who has used a calorie tracker knows the moment. It's nine in the evening, the number has turned red, and the app has only one thing to say: how far over you are. It doesn't ask whether you were stressed, or why lunch never happened. So the next morning you don't open it. Fimbl began with a simple question: what would a food diary look like if it were built for that evening?

In short

Fimbl is a calm food diary for iPhone and iPad built around three steps: notice what you eat, understand why, and change one small thing at a time.

  • It tracks calories like any good counter, then asks about hunger and feelings too.
  • Numbers are optional, the free version is a complete diary, and there are no ads.
  • Our end goal: show you your patterns, not an average person's.

The problem with counting alone

Calorie trackers are good at what they were designed for. They answer what you ate and how much. Keeping that kind of record is genuinely useful: reviews of weight-management research link consistent food logging with better results.2

But "consistent" is doing a lot of work in that sentence. In one study of young adults who used healthy-eating and fitness apps, almost half described negative experiences, including guilt and a sense of being controlled.1 An app that makes you feel bad is an app you stop opening, and a diary you don't open can't help you.

We think the gap is simple. Most apps only ever ask about the food. Eating is rarely only about food.

Notice, understand, change

Everything in Fimbl serves one loop. It's small on purpose, because small is what you can repeat on a busy Tuesday.

  1. NoticeLog a meal in seconds, then a ten-second check-in: how hungry were you, and what were you feeling?
  2. UnderstandOver days and weeks, those check-ins turn into patterns you can actually see, for you and nobody else.
  3. ChangeOne gentle focus at a time. Not a new diet, just the next small step that fits your week.

Mindful eating research supports the first half of that loop most clearly. Reviews find mindfulness-based approaches are most effective for emotional and external-cue eating, the kind of eating that happens when the reason isn't hunger.3 Noticing comes first.

Six decisions that shaped Fimbl

Building a calm app is mostly a series of small refusals. These are the decisions we keep coming back to.

  • Feelings belong at the table Every meal can come with a quick check-in on hunger and feelings, and it's free. Craving, stressed, bored, satisfied: pick as many as you like, or none.
  • Numbers are optional Calm mode (Premium) swaps calorie numbers for gentle grades on how well a meal served your goals, hunger and mood, while counting quietly in the background.
  • Free means a complete diary Search, barcode scanning, saved meals, check-ins, weight, goals, Apple Health activity and progress photos all work for free. No ads, ever.
  • AI with the lights on AI is optional and Premium-only. It sees only what a feature needs, never your name or email, and we don't train models on your data.4
  • Your photos are yours Progress photos stay on your iPhone. They're never uploaded to our servers, never used for AI and never included in iCloud backups.
  • Consistency, not perfection Streaks reward showing up, not flawless days. Our rule of thumb is printed right in the app: miss a day? That's fine. Just don't miss two.

  • A guided first weekSeven days, one small step each, with a nudge at the time you choose.

  • Gentle streaksMeals, mood, mindful eating and steps, with consistency celebrated.

  • Private progress photosLined up the same way every time, kept only on your iPhone.

What we deliberately left out

Some features would probably grow an app faster. We decided they would make Fimbl worse at its job.

Not in Fimbl

Ads and ad trackers
Your diary is not an ad inventory, in the free version either.
Leaderboards and public feeds
Comparing plates with strangers doesn't build a calmer relationship with food.
"Bad food" labels
Grades describe how a meal fit your goals and mood, never your worth.
Streak shame
No guilt trips for a missed day. You can even restore a streak with Premium.

What we built instead

A quiet, private space
Delete your account any time from Settings.
Your own story
Weekly reflections about your week: wins, patterns and one gentle focus.
Context
Hunger and feelings next to the food, so a hard day makes sense.
Kind defaults
Nudges at the time you choose, and a first week you can skip or replay.

Rebuilt from the ground up

Fimbl 2.0 was a complete rewrite, built natively for iPhone and iPad. That made room for the things that make a diary feel effortless: Home and Lock Screen widgets, Siri and Shortcuts for hands-free logging ("Log my weight in Fimbl"), Apple Health for steps, energy and workouts, light and dark mode, and a full translation into German and Finnish alongside English. Version 2.1 added guided progress photos that never leave your phone.

Fimbl is designed and built in Espoo, Finland. If something feels off, tell us: feedback genuinely shapes what we build next.

Where we're going: your patterns, not averages

Here's the part we're most excited about. Food apps know what you ate. Mood apps know how you felt. Almost nobody connects the two on the same timeline as your weight, your movement and your week.

Almost every app can tell you what you ate. We want to help you understand why, in a way that's true for you.

Fimbl already collects the pieces: meals, hunger, feelings, daily mood, weight and activity, all in one calm place. The end goal is to turn them into insight that belongs to you alone. Think of observations like "on days with a morning walk, you felt more energised", or noticing that a rushed lunch tends to come before a hungry evening. Not rules about food, just your own patterns, gently pointed out.

From there, the loop closes. Instead of a generic programme, you'd get one tiny experiment for the week, drawn from your own data, and see your own result at the end of it. We'll share that work here on the blog as it ships, without promising dates we can't keep.

Who Fimbl is for, and who it isn't

Fimbl is for you if you've tried counting before and burned out, if you're curious why you reach for food when you're not hungry, or if you simply want a food diary that feels kind. It suits beginners and people who already know their way around macros.

It's not the right tool if you need Android or a web app, if you track specific micronutrients in detail, or if you need clinical support. In those cases, the apps in our comparison or a healthcare professional will serve you better. And if you're wondering whether a craving is hunger or something else, our guide to emotional vs physical hunger is a good place to start.
