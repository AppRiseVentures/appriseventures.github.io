## How we compared them

Feature lists are easy to compare and tell you surprisingly little. So alongside the usual checks, we looked at what decides whether a tracker actually helps:

- **Speed:** how many ways you can log a meal, and how fast each one is.
- **What's free:** whether the basics (search, barcode, history) work without paying, and whether the free version shows ads.
- **The off days:** how the app treats you when you go over, skip a day or eat a birthday cake.
- **Insight beyond calories:** whether it helps you notice hunger, feelings and patterns, not just totals.
- **Privacy and reach:** ads, data use, platforms and languages.

The last three matter more than they sound. Reviews of weight-management research consistently find that people who keep logging tend to do better, but the evidence is about *consistency*.[13] The tracker that wins is the one you keep opening.

## What each app is genuinely best at

- Best for the biggest database

### MyFitnessPal

The default name in calorie counting, with more than 20 million foods and links to dozens of apps and devices. Since December 2025 it also owns Cal AI.

**Keep in mind:** barcode scanning, ad-free use and custom macro goals are part of Premium, and the free version shows ads.

- Best for micronutrients

### Cronometer

The precision tool. It tracks 84 nutrients for free from curated, lab-based data, which makes it a favourite of dietitians and anyone managing specific nutrients.

**Keep in mind:** the free version shows ads, and the level of detail can feel like a lab report if you simply want to eat a little better.

- Best for adaptive macros

### MacroFactor

Its algorithm estimates your energy expenditure from your weight trend and adjusts your targets every week, without scolding you for imperfect days. Great for training goals.

**Keep in mind:** there's no free tier (a 7-day trial), no web app, and the app is in English and Japanese only.

- Best for a simple calorie budget

### Lose It!

A straightforward daily budget that has been helping people count since 2008. Clean, familiar and easy to start.

**Keep in mind:** its App Store listing puts barcode scanning and AI logging under Premium, and the free version shows ads.

- Best for European foods

### YAZIO

A German app with a large food database, a free barcode scanner and popular fasting timers, available in 20 languages.

**Keep in mind:** the free version shows ads, and photo logging, the mood diary and most fasting plans are part of PRO. There's no web version.

- Best for meal plans

### Lifesum

A design-led Swedish app built around healthy eating, with diet programmes, recipes, a free barcode scanner and a simple "Life Score".

**Keep in mind:** AI tracking, custom macro goals and meal plans are Premium, and micronutrient detail is limited.

- Best for photo-only logging

### Cal AI

Point your camera, get calories and macros. If typing a single food name feels like too much, it's the fastest route from plate to log.

**Keep in mind:** scan results require a subscription, and it tracks calories and macros only, with nothing on hunger or mood.

- Best for a guided programme

### Noom

Less a tracker than a structured programme, with daily psychology lessons and coaching. Foods are sorted by a colour system based on calorie density.

**Keep in mind:** it's built around a subscription programme, and its GLP-1 medication offering (Noom Med) is available in the US only.

- Best for a habit that lasts, and understanding why you eat

### Fimbl

A calm food diary for iPhone and iPad that tracks calories and macros like the others, then adds the part they leave out: how hungry you were, how you felt, and what that means for you. The free version is a complete diary with a free barcode scanner and no ads. More on why we rank it first below.

**Keep in mind:** iPhone and iPad only, no micronutrient deep-dive, and macro goals and AI features are part of Premium.

## The question most comparisons skip

Most "best calorie app" lists rank database size and AI accuracy. Those matter. But if you've tried tracking before, you probably didn't stop because a food was missing. You stopped because it started to feel like a test you kept failing.

The research backs this up in both directions. Logging what you eat is one of the most reliable habits linked to weight-management success, and more consistent digital tracking goes with better results.[13, 14] Yet in one study, almost half of young adults using healthy-eating and fitness apps described negative experiences such as guilt and feeling controlled.[15] On the other hand, a randomised trial found that a month of calorie tracking didn't raise eating-disorder risk in low-risk students.[16] The takeaway isn't that tracking is bad. It's that **how an app makes you feel is part of whether it works**.

The best tracker isn't the one with the most foods. It's the one you still open on the day you'd rather not.

## Where Fimbl isn't the best choice

Every app has limits, Fimbl included. Choose something else if:

- **You use Android or want a web app.** Fimbl is iPhone and iPad only. MyFitnessPal, Cronometer and Lose It! run on both, and YAZIO and Lifesum on Android.
- **You track specific vitamins and minerals.** Cronometer's micronutrient detail is in a different league.
- **You're dialling in macros for training.** MacroFactor's adaptive targets are built for exactly that.
- **You eat out at many US chains.** MyFitnessPal's database is hard to beat for branded and restaurant items.
- **You need medical support.** No app replaces a doctor or dietitian. Fimbl isn't a medical tool or a treatment for eating disorders.

## How to choose in two minutes

1. **Start with your phone.** On Android or the web, narrow it to MyFitnessPal, Cronometer, Lose It!, YAZIO or Lifesum. On iPhone, everything here is open to you.
2. **Name what you're really after.** Precision (micronutrients, macros for training) points to Cronometer or MacroFactor. A calmer relationship with food and habits that last points to Fimbl.
3. **Notice how numbers make you feel.** If seeing a calorie total every day motivates you, any counter works. If it makes you anxious, pick an app that lets you step back from numbers without losing track.
4. **Check what's free before you commit.** Make sure the basics you'll use daily, like barcode scanning, aren't behind a paywall, and that the free version doesn't fill your diary with ads.

Then give it a real week. Not a perfect one, a real one. The right app is the one that's still on your home screen when the week is over.

## Questions, answered

### What is the best calorie tracker app in 2026?

It depends on what you need. For most people who want a lasting habit without guilt, we recommend Fimbl, because it pairs quick logging with hunger and feelings check-ins and an optional numbers-free Calm mode. Cronometer is the best choice for detailed micronutrients, MacroFactor for adaptive macro targets, and MyFitnessPal for the largest food database.

### Which calorie counter has a free barcode scanner?

As of October 2026, Fimbl, Cronometer, YAZIO and Lifesum include barcode scanning in their free versions. MyFitnessPal moved barcode scanning to Premium in 2022, and Lose It! lists it as a Premium feature in its App Store description.

### Is there a calorie tracker without ads?

Fimbl has no ads in either its free or Premium version. MacroFactor never uses ads but has no free tier, and Lifesum and Cal AI list no ads. MyFitnessPal, Cronometer, Lose It! and YAZIO show ads in their free versions and remove them with a paid plan.

### Can I track what I eat without seeing calories?

Yes. Fimbl's Calm mode (Premium) replaces calorie numbers with gentle A to C grades for how well each meal served your goals, hunger and mood, while it keeps counting quietly in the background. You can switch back to numbers at any time. None of the other apps in this comparison document a comparable mode.

### What is a good alternative to MyFitnessPal?

On iPhone, Fimbl is a good alternative if you want free barcode scanning, no ads and a calmer, feelings-aware diary. Cronometer suits people who want micronutrient detail, and YAZIO suits people who want a large European food database on Android as well as iPhone.

### Is calorie counting bad for your mental health?

For many people it's a useful, neutral tool. Some studies link calorie-tracking apps with more eating concern in certain users, while a randomised trial in low-risk students found no increase in eating-disorder risk. If tracking makes you anxious, a numbers-optional approach can help, and if food feels overwhelming, please talk to a healthcare professional.

### Does Fimbl work on Android?

No. Fimbl is built natively for iPhone and iPad running iOS or iPadOS 26 or later. If you need Android or a web app, MyFitnessPal, Cronometer, Lose It!, YAZIO and Lifesum are available there.
